# Last Call — Rules

A card game for 2–8 people around a table, played on your phones. Everybody
picks a drink, everybody gets a hand of cards, and the last one still
standing wins.

Your **glass is your wallet.** Cards cost pulls — sips — and the only way to
pay is to drink. Refilling means finishing what's in front of you.

> **Play responsibly.** Every cost in this game is a real sip. Set a handicap
> (below), pick a soft drink, or sit a round out. Nobody at the table needs
> to know why.

---

## The short version

1. Pick a drink. That's your deck and your budget.
2. Each round: draw, talk, secretly stage your cards, reveal, resolve.
3. Playing a card costs pulls. Pulls come out of your glass.
4. Empty glass? Refill it and draw five new cards.
5. Drop to 0 HP and you're out — but you're not gone.
6. Last one standing wins.

---

## Setting up

**Everybody joins the room** and picks a **vessel** — what you're drinking
and what it's in ("can", "pint", "glass"). Your vessel decides two things:

| Deck | Style | Pulls per refill | Cost range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beer** | Attrition — steady, unglamorous damage | 8 | 1–2 |
| **Cider** | Trickster — chip damage and pull drains | 10 | 1–3 |
| **Wine** | Control — curses that tick every round | 6 | 2–3 |
| **Liquor** | Burst — expensive, hits hard | 4 | 2–3 |
| **Soft** | Support — shields and healing | 6 | 1–2 |

"Pulls per refill" is how many sips your glass holds. Liquor gets 4 — a shot
glass empties fast, but its cards hit hardest per pull. Cider gets 10 — a
pint of cider is a long, cheap game.

You can hold **more than one vessel**. A card can only be paid for with pulls
from *its own* deck's glass, so a second drink means a second budget and a
second set of cards.

**Handicap** (optional, lobby only). A percentage — 100 is normal, 25 is a
quarter price, 300 is triple. Set it lower if you're driving, pacing
yourself, or just smaller than the person next to you. It scales every card
you play, rounded up. Nobody has to explain their number.

Everyone starts at **15 HP**.

### The lobby

Round 1's draw beat is the lobby. While you're in it you can swap vessels,
set your handicap, and **mulligan as much as you like** — throw cards back
and draw new ones until you're happy. Once the game starts, mulligans drop to
one per round.

---

## A round

Five beats. The table moves as one — a beat ends when everyone has done their
bit, not on a timer.

### 1. DRAW

Optional, and once per round. Tap **finish & draw** to empty your glass, fill
it back to full, and draw **5 new cards** from that deck.

That's the trade: cards cost drinking, and getting more cards costs drinking
faster. You never *have* to draw. You just run out of cards if you don't.

You may also **mulligan** once — throw any number of cards back and draw the
same number again from the same decks. The cards you throw go onto that deck's
discard pile, so you won't see them again until the deck runs out and gets
reshuffled — unless it's already running low, in which case you might.

### 2. DEAL

Automatic. The round's **event** is revealed (see below) and anyone who
settled a tab gets a new one.

### 3. DIPLOMACY

The talking beat, and the one the game is actually about.

Stage your cards face down — pick a card, pick a target. Nobody can see what
you've staged, only that you've **locked in**. Argue, threaten, promise, lie.
The beat ends when everyone has locked.

You can also **offer a pact** here: a secret two-player alliance. Pacts stay
hidden. If the two of you are the last ones standing, **you both win**. If
one of you attacks the other, the betrayal becomes public and the betrayer is
barred from ever pacting again.

### 4. REVEAL

Everything flips at once. Pulls are charged **now** — this is where you
actually drink for what you staged.

Then the **reactions** window. A reaction is a card you play *in response* to
a revealed play, and it's public the moment you play it:

- **Cancel** — the play does nothing. The pulls stay spent.
- **Reduce** — the play deals 3 or 4 less damage to you.
- **Reflect** — the damage goes back at whoever sent it.

Reactions can't be reacted to.

**Ghosts vote here.** If you're out, you get one **haunt** per round: pick any
damage play in flight and add +1 to it. You have no cards and no HP, but you
still get a say in who wins.

### 5. RESOLVE

Automatic, and in a strict order: **the biggest spender goes first.** Spend
more pulls, act earlier. Ties break by seat position.

Then, in order: damage and healing land, shields absorb, curses tick,
anybody at 0 HP is eliminated, effects expire, hands trim to 12 cards and
tabs pay out.

**Then the round stops and shows you the damage.** THE ROUND lists everything
that just happened — who played what at whom, and what it did. It includes
the things you would otherwise never see: a hit your shield ate whole, a card
that fizzled because its target was already out, a play somebody cancelled out
from under its owner.

Anyone the round touched has to tap **GOT IT** before it rolls over. If
nothing landed on you, you do not have to tap — but you can, and it costs
nothing. A round where nothing happened at all does not stop.

## Cards

Every card names its deck, its cost in pulls, and who it can hit.

| Kind | What it does |
|---|---|
| **ATK** | Damage. One target or the whole table. |
| **BUFF** | Healing or shields, usually on yourself. |
| **CURSE** | A dot — damage that ticks every round until it expires. |
| **UTIL** | Pull drains and odds and ends. |
| **REACTION** | Played in the reveal window, against a revealed play. |
| **CHALLENGE** | A real-life contest. See below. |

**Shields** absorb damage before your HP does. They don't stack — a new
shield replaces the old one, so there's no banking them.

**Curses** never tick in the round they're cast. They start the round after.

**Healing has no ceiling.** You can go above 15. A big heal is a real
comeback, not a wasted card.

### Challenge cards

A challenge is a contest you settle **in real life**, and the table votes on
the winner.

- **Duel** — you against the player on your right.
- **Solo** — you perform the card's task; the table votes pass or fail.

The game pauses until the votes are in. Losers pay in damage, drained pulls,
sips, or a **personal rule** they carry for a few rounds — a real rule, at the
real table, enforced by real people.

## Events

One per round, revealed at the deal, affecting everybody:

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| **HAPPY HOUR** | Every card costs half, rounded up. |
| **LAST ORDERS** | Play at least one card or take 2 damage. |
| **TOAST** | Everyone drinks 1 and heals 2. |
| **DOUBLE VISION** | Every attack hits the player left of its target. |
| **BIG SHOT** | The round's biggest spender takes 2 damage. |
| **HOUSE POUR** | Every curse ticks double. |
| **ON THE HOUSE** | Everyone heals 2 at the end of the round. |

You'll see all seven before any repeats, and never the same one twice in a
row.

## Tabs

A private objective, dealt to you at the start and replaced whenever you
settle one. Nobody else sees what you're chasing — only, a round later, that
you settled *something*.

Examples: play no cards for a whole round (LIE LOW), play three or more in
one round (SHOWBOAT), finish a vessel and draw (BOTTOMS UP), spend four or
more pulls in a round (HIGH ROLLER). They pay out in HP or pulls.

Seven tabs, and you'll cycle through all of them before seeing one twice.

---

## Getting knocked out

At 0 HP you're **eliminated**. Your hand is discarded, your effects are
cleared, and your tab is gone.

You are not, however, done. As a **ghost** you can haunt one damage play per
round, adding +1 to it, and you still vote on challenges. The table has to
keep living with you.

## Winning

- **Last player standing** wins.
- **The last two standing, still pacted,** both win. Pacts pay off.
- **Everybody eliminated in the same round** is a draw.

When it ends, everything comes out: the pacts, the betrayals, the tabs, and
the full stats board.

---

## What the table can and can't see

The game is built around a strict line between public and private. It is
worth knowing exactly where that line sits, because a lot of the bluffing
depends on it.

**Public** — HP, how full your glass is, how many cards are in your hand
(and which decks they're from — but never *which* cards), whether you've
locked in, how many cards are left in each deck, the discard piles, every
revealed play, every reaction, every haunt, every betrayal, the round log.

**Private** — the cards in your hand, anything you've staged but not
revealed, your tab, and every pact that hasn't been broken.

**Announced after the fact** — that somebody settled a tab, a round later,
without saying which one.

Two things worth knowing: hand *counts* by deck are public, so a sharp
opponent can tell you're holding four Liquor cards even if not which four.
And unlisted things are "not listed", not "encrypted" — this is a party
game, not a security product. Play with people you'd hand your phone to.

## House rules

Everything here is tunable in the code, and most of it is meant to be:

- **Starting HP** is 15.
- **Hand cap** is 12 — over that, your newest cards are discarded at the end
  of the round.
- **Draw** is 5 cards per finish-and-draw.
- **Handicap** ranges 25%–300%.
- **Pacts** need at least 4 players still alive to be offered.
- **Haunts** add +1 damage, once per ghost per round.

If a rule is getting in the way of your table having a good time, change it.
That's what it's for.
