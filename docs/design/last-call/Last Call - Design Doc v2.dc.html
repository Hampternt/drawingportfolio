Drinks · Last Call Rules spec v2 · working document

Rules & mechanics

The implementable definition of Last Call: objects, state, the round loop, resolution order, and the rules an engine has to enforce. Written to be argued with and edited — every number that is not settled carries a TBD-n tag and appears in §13. The pitch and the walkthrough live in their own files; nothing here is written to persuade.

0 · How to read this

Rule numbers are stable

Cite them in tickets and card text (“see 6.3”). Renumber only when a section is deleted outright.

MUST / MAY

MUST is engine-enforced. MAY is a player choice the UI has to offer. Anything else is intent, not law.

Design-intent notes

One per section, in violet. They explain why a rule exists so you know what you are breaking when you change it.

Intent. Vessels are first-class rather than a number on the player, because multi-deck play (§4.4) and per-deck draw only work if each drink has its own budget and its own pile.

2 · Setup

  1. 2.1 Seating. Players join by room code and are assigned seats in join order. Seat order is the tie-break for everything downstream (§7.2) and MUST NOT change mid-game. 2–8 players; 7 is the display ceiling for one ring on the big screen.
  2. 2.2 Deck and container. Each player registers what they are drinking. The deck follows from the ABV band (§3.1); the container sets pulls_max from the table in §3.2. The app MUST NOT ask the player to do arithmetic — it states the pull count and marks the next pull on a gauge.
  3. 2.3 Handicap. A per-player cost multiplier set at seating and visible to everyone (§11).
  4. 2.4 Starting HP. Every player starts at 15 regardless of deck or handicap. TBD-1
  5. 2.5 Opening hand. Five cards per registered vessel TBD-4, drawn from that vessel's deck. A two-vessel player therefore opens on ten cards and a fuller rail — paid for by having to finish two drinks to refill.
  6. 2.6 Hidden state. One tab is dealt privately per player (§10.2); pacts are assigned silently (§10.3). The first event is dealt at beat 2 of round 1, not at setup.

Intent. Everything asymmetric about a player is declared before the first card is dealt, in public, except the tab. Surprises come from play, not from setup.

3 · Decks and the pour standard

3.1 A vessel is a serving of alcohol, not a volume, so the decks balance against each other. Each deck states the band it is balanced for; a drink outside the band belongs to the deck whose band it falls in, and the app MUST say so at pick time.

Deck Band Vessel · pulls Cost spread Role
Beer0.5–7%50cl · 81–2Attrition. No bomb in the list.
Cider0.5–7%50cl · 101–3Trickster. Redirect, swap, theft.
Wine7–20%15cl · 62–3Control. Delays and damage over time.
Liquor20%+4cl · 42–3Burst. Four pulls is two plays and a decision.
Soft0–0.5%any · 61–2Support. Shields, heals, sabotage. No alcohol cost.

3.2 Pull count is a deck constant. A Beer vessel is 8 pulls whether the tin is 50cl or 25cl — the app converts to a fraction of the container. What a player can spend never depends on what was in the fridge.

3.3 Cocktails and jugs. A drink built on more than one measure is several vessels, not a big one. A four-measure jug is four Liquor vessels and refilling means finishing one glass of it.

3.4 Low-and-no. Anything under 0.5% is Soft regardless of what the label imitates.

Intent. Soft must be pickable by someone who could drink. If it ever reads as a penalty the balance is wrong, not the player.

4 · The vessel economy

4.1 Pulls are the only resource. A card's cost is paid in pulls of its own deck, taken from a vessel of that deck. Paying a pull is an instruction to drink; the app tracks the count, the player does the drinking, and nothing verifies it.

  • 4.2 Playability gate. A card MAY be armed only if cost × handicap ≤ pulls_left on a vessel of its deck at the moment of locking. Level gates what you can play, never what you can hold.
  • 4.3 Finish & draw. At beat 1 a player MAY finish any one vessel: empty it, open a fresh one of the same deck, and draw five cards TBD-4 from that deck on top of the hand they keep. The alternative is KEEP — drink nothing, draw nothing, and go into the round on what is left. These are the only two draw rules in the game.
  • 4.4 More than one drink. A player MAY register additional vessels at any round boundary. Each is its own deck, its own pulls_left and its own draw source; a card MUST be paid from a vessel of its own deck. Finish & draw applies to one vessel per round TBD-5. Abandoning a vessel means finishing it first.
  • 4.5 Empty vessels. A vessel at zero pulls is dead weight: its cards stay in hand and stay unplayable until it is finished and refilled. The UI MUST show that as the reason a card is greyed, not as a generic block.

Intent. One decision carries the whole economy: drink now for cards, or stay dry and play what you have. Every earlier mechanic that also spent drink (racks, allocators, spill) has been removed so this choice is unambiguous.

6 · Arming, targeting, locking

  • 6.1 Arming moves a card from hand to armed[]. It is reversible until lock and costs nothing.
  • 6.2 Targeting. A card declares its target class in targets: self | one | all | table. A play with an illegal or dead target at resolution is discarded with no effect and its pulls are NOT refunded (7.5).
  • 6.3 Locking is final. The engine revalidates every armed card against 4.2 at lock time and rejects the lock if any card fails, naming the card.
  • 6.4 Payment happens at reveal, not at lock, so a card cancelled during the response window still costs its pulls. Drinking is not refundable.

Intent. Paying at reveal means a bluffed lock is expensive. It also means the pull count is public information the moment plays flip, which is what feeds the ordering rule.

7 · Resolution and timing

  • 7.1 Order key. Plays resolve in descending order of pulls spent by their player this round. Spending most acts first.
  • 7.2 Ties break by seat order starting from first_seat, which advances one seat per round. Within one player's plays, the player's own arming order applies.
  • 7.3 Reactions. REACTION cards are the only cards playable outside beat 4. Played during the response window, they resolve immediately before the play they answer, last-in-first-out per targeted play, and MUST name a play as their target.
  • 7.4 One pass. There is no second response window. A reaction cannot be reacted to TBD-7.
  • 7.5 Fizzle. A play whose target is gone, already cancelled, or otherwise illegal resolves as nothing. It still counts toward the ordering total and still cost its pulls.
  • 7.6 State check. HP is evaluated after every play resolves, not at the end of the beat. Elimination is immediate and removes that player's unresolved plays from the queue.

Intent. Ordering by spend makes drinking a tempo currency as well as a resource, and gives the table something to read during diplomacy: whoever is committing hardest also goes first.

8 · Hand

  • 8.1 No hard cap. A hand may exceed the comfortable display size; the wheel UI is endless and does not constrain it.
  • 8.2 Soft cap. At the end of beat 6 a player holding more than 12 cards MUST discard down to 12, choosing which. TBD-2
  • 8.3 Multi-deck hands are one hand, not one per vessel. The rail groups by deck colour so the split is legible.
  • 8.4 Discards go to the discard pile of the card's own deck. An empty deck reshuffles its discard at beat 1 and the big screen announces it.

Intent. The soft cap exists to stop hoarding, not to punish a good draw — it bites once a round, at a moment when nobody is waiting on you.

9 · Damage, elimination, end of game

  • 9.1 HP starts at 15 (2.4), has no ceiling from healing TBD-3, and is public at all times.
  • 9.2 Zero is out. At 0 HP the player is eliminated, finishes what is left in their vessels, and becomes a ghost: one vote per round to add +1 damage to any attack already in flight. Ghosts hold no cards and cannot be targeted.
  • 9.3 The game ends when one player remains, or when all remaining players are ghosts (draw). Expect 5–8 rounds at 15 HP.

10 · Persistent systems

10.1 Events

Public, table-wide, exactly one round, dealt at beat 2 before diplomacy so it can be negotiated around. Never two at once. Guardrail: an event MUST NOT change how much anyone drinks beyond one extra pull.

10.2 Tabs

Private objectives, one per player, dealt at seating and replaced on completion. Completion is engine-detected and announced after the fact, never before.

10.3 Pacts

Two players are silently paired at setup and dealt matching tabs. Neither is told who the other is. Build this first — it is the cheapest system with the biggest effect on beat 3.

10.4 Effects from cards (curses, shields, rules) live on the room with an expires_round and are applied in the order they were created. Two effects of the same keyword on the same subject do not stack unless the keyword says so TBD-8.

11 · Handicap

A per-player multiplier on card cost in pulls, set at seating and public. It is the lever for people who want to play the same game at a different rate: pace yourself, drive, or take a heavier share. It MUST NOT touch HP, damage, or draw — only what a card costs to play, rounded up.

Intent. One dial, in the open, so nobody has to explain themselves twice. Because it multiplies cost and cost drives turn order (7.1), a heavy handicap also buys tempo — check that this is not dominant in playtest.

12 · Adjudications

  • Disconnect at lock. A player who does not lock plays nothing and drinks nothing. Their armed cards return to hand.
  • Two reactions to one play. Resolve LIFO. If both cancel it, the second cancels a play that is already cancelled and fizzles (7.5).
  • Damage from an eliminated player. Plays already resolved stand; unresolved plays are removed (7.6).
  • Empty deck mid-round. Draws pause until beat 1 of the next round, when the discard reshuffles. Nobody draws a partial hand.
  • Refusing to drink. Never enforced and never surfaced. The count moves on; the app does not police anybody.

14 · Engine notes

State machine

SETUP → DRAW → DEAL → DIPLOMACY → LOCK → REVEAL → RESOLVE → (DRAW | GAME_OVER)

One authoritative transition per beat, server-side, driven by ready-count or timer. Clients never advance state; they render it and post intents.

Invariants to assert
  • 0 ≤ pulls_left ≤ pulls_max for every vessel.
  • Every card in hand or armed has a vessel of its deck registered.
  • Cards in play + hands + discards = deck size, per deck.
  • No play survives its own resolution; the queue empties every round.

Transport. Reuses the existing room shell: SSE for state pushes, the same auth, one connection. Screens: phone GAME tab (hand wheel, cost rail), phone TABLE tab (mini felt), and the big screen at /room/{code}/screen. Screen behaviour is specified in Last Call — Game UI, not here.
