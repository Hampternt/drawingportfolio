The TV shows order, not arcs

Attack arcs across a board look good and read badly. Since turn order now matters, the centre column is the resolution stack itself — numbered, top to bottom, with responses lifted above the card they answer.

Nothing secret ever reaches it

Quest cards show only as a violet QUEST chip and a count. Hand contents never appear, except a card someone chose to REVEAL in diplomacy — which is exactly why revealing is worth doing.

Readable from a sofa

Nothing below 11px at 1080p, HP at 38px, phase at 56px, and the timer in mono so digits don't jitter. Lock lights sit next to the beat name because “who are we waiting for” is the only question the TV must answer instantly.

Open, from designing this

A 16-card hand at 4 columns leaves room for a name and four words of rules text. Either card effects stay that terse, or a tap-to-enlarge overlay is mandatory. Also: with per-player decks, a 5-player table needs five deck counters — the bottom rail caps at six before it needs paging.
